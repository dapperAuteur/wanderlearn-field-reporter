## Block 3 — Word-for-word VO (verbatim)

**[Beat 1 · talking-head · 0:00]**
> The second failure mode is subtler than vagueness, because each criterion looks fine
> on its own. Overlapping criteria test the same underlying property more than once, so
> a single defect gets counted twice.

**[Beat 2 · slide: three phrasings · 0:30]**
> Names the customer's problem. Shows the customer they were understood. Addresses the
> issue raised. Read them again. They are three phrasings of one check: did the reply
> engage with the actual problem. A draft that names the cracked blender passes all
> three. A draft that does not fails all three. They covary.

**[Beat 3 · slide: the two breakages · 1:15]**
> Overlap quietly breaks both halves of the critic. It distorts a weighted score: if
> three of five criteria are secretly the same check, that one property is worth sixty
> percent of the score and you never decided that. And it triples the feedback: the
> writer gets three failing checks that all say the same thing, over-corrects on that
> axis, and ignores the checks that appeared once.

**[Beat 4 · talking-head · 2:10]**
> And it is dangerous precisely because each criterion passes the vagueness test. Every
> one is observable. The defect is in the relationship between them, which makes it the
> easiest failure mode to ship without noticing.

**[Beat 5 · slide: independence test · 2:40]**
> The fix: criteria should be independent. The test: can you imagine a draft that passes
> this one and fails that one, and vice versa? Acknowledges-issue and gives-next-step
> are independent: a reply can name the problem yet offer no action. Gives-next-step and
> states-timeline are nearly independent, which is why timeline is a half-weight nudge,
> so the near-overlap cannot double-count as two blocking checks.

**[Beat 6 · slide: mirror diagram · 3:20]**
> Do not confuse this with the next lesson. Overlap is two criteria testing one
> property. Compound is one criterion testing two. Mirror images. Both fixed by the same
> north star: one criterion, one independent, observable property.
